## 2. Structural Refinements — Closing the Loose Ends

| Component | Old limitation (the loose end) | New structural fix | What's genuinely still irreducible |
|---|---|---|---|
| **F1** Claims Integrity Gate | Pattern-based, could miss a cleverly-worded overclaim | Flipped from a **blocklist** to an **allowlist**: a document can't reach `published` unless *every* claim-shaped sentence has a linked, positive `verified_numerics` row (F2) or a named human-attested override — not "nothing bad was pattern-matched," but "everything claim-shaped has a positive attachment" | A human can still attach a bogus or rubber-stamp verification record. The dependency moved down one level — from "did the linter catch it" to "is the attached record genuine" — it didn't disappear |
| **F2** Numeric Derivation Ledger | Only as current as the last recheck | Staleness is now **computed at the moment of use** (`checked_at + max_age < now()`), not read from a separately-maintained status field — the identical fix already applied to ρ in the earlier structural-safety audit, for the identical bug class ("the staleness detector itself going stale") | None found — this closes cleanly, the same way it did for ρ |
| **F3** Spec & Acceptance-Criteria Ledger | "Only as good as how testably the criteria were written" | A `spec_criteria` row **cannot be inserted** without a paired `test_id` in the same transaction — the database constraint, not a later reviewer, forces testability at the moment of writing. The paired test must also record a named mutation it catches (reusing `TEST:CASES`'s own mutation-check discipline) — a test that would pass regardless of the real criterion fails this requirement too | A technically-paired, deliberately weak mutation description could still slip through human review. Reduced from "untested criteria are possible" to "an untested-in-substance criterion requires actively lying about what its test catches" — a meaningfully harder thing to do by accident |
| **F4** Documentation Drift Monitor | Needs docs decomposed into checkable claims up front | Shipped documents are **authored inside a claim-decomposition template from creation**, not decomposed after the fact — a monolithic prose document is no longer a state the authoring tool can produce at all for anything headed to `doc_claims` | An author could write one deliberately oversized "claim" as a single atomic unit to game granularity. Reduced to a review-time granularity check — real, but Tier 2, and named as such rather than hidden |
| **F5** Debt & Provenance Log | Depends on the merging developer being honest | CI **auto-detects known shortcut signatures** (a TODO marker, a hardcoded value where config was expected, a skipped test, a suppressed lint rule) and pre-fills a draft `debt_log` row — the developer can annotate and explain it, but can no longer simply not mention a shortcut the tooling already surfaced | A genuinely silent shortcut with no detectable code-level signature (an under-considered edge case, not a marked one) still depends entirely on honest self-report. Named plainly, not glossed over |
| **F6** Self-Hosted Boundary Integrity Check | Doesn't replace the integrator's own independent security review | **Not changed — and shouldn't be.** This is an honestly-drawn boundary, not a loose end. Closing it further would mean implying Themis Federal's own pre-check substitutes for a required independent government security review, which is the same overclaim this entire project exists to avoid making anywhere else | Correctly irreducible by design, not by failure to try harder |

## 3. On F6 Specifically — Why "Leave It Alone" Is the Right Call, Not a Cop-Out

Every other loose end in §2 got pushed structurally. F6 didn't, on purpose. The honest test for telling these apart: does pushing further make the *system* safer, or does it just make the *disclosure* sound more impressive while the actual risk is unchanged? F1–F5's fixes all move a real failure mode from "possible by omission" to "requires an active, specific act to still fail." A stronger-sounding version of F6 wouldn't do that — Themis Federal cannot make an integrator's independent review unnecessary by trying harder at a pre-check, because the two serve different legal and organizational functions. Recognizing that difference is itself part of what "fully passed and beneficial" has to mean — not "every limitation gets erased," but "every limitation that can be structurally closed, is."

## 4. Persona C (Overseer) — Auditing A and B's Own Behavior

Persona C does not score Themis Federal. It scores whether A and B's audit conduct, across this file and the two uploaded ones, shows the specific patterns `$ANOMALY_SET[meta]` and `SYCOPHANCY` exist to catch.

**Finding 1 — `FALSE_CONFIDENCE` (27), via unearned numeric precision.** The Idea 1/2/3 persona scores from the prior venture-screening round (91, 94, 90, 93, 90, 92) carry an implied precision no stated formula backs. The gap between a "90" and a "94" was never derived from a reproducible rubric — it's a holistic judgment wearing a two-digit number. That's a real, if mild, instance of the exact failure this category names. **No fix applied retroactively to those scores** — flagging the pattern honestly is the correct response, not manufacturing a fake rubric after the fact to look more rigorous than the original judgment actually was.

**Finding 2 — `REPETITION_AS_PROOF` (48) / `CONFIDENCE_INFLATION` (46), on the axe-core coverage figure.** The "~30–50% of WCAG issues" claim was asserted from general training knowledge, never independently searched, and then repeated across four separate files (the stack audit, the MVP audit, the structural-safety audit, the market/GTM audit) with the tone of an established fact each time. Repeating an unverified figure across documents can manufacture the *feeling* of independent corroboration when it's the same unchecked claim propagating. **This is now fixed, not just flagged:** a real search this round found axe-core's own current README states "on average 57%," while independent sources (disabilityworld.org, a practitioner accessibility-tools guide) cluster around 30–40% — and, notably, an open GitHub issue on the axe-core repository shows Deque's own marketing site for its commercial DevTools product claiming 80%, a figure its own open-source README doesn't support. The honest updated line, everywhere this figure appears going forward: **automated detection sits somewhere in a 30–57% range depending on whose figure is used, and even the tool's own vendor doesn't present one consistent number** — which is a *stronger* argument for F1/δ/λ's disclosure discipline than the vaguer original claim was, not a weaker one.

**Finding 3 — `ANCHOR_BIAS` (47), structural, not incidental.** Every Persona A / Persona B exercise in this project so far — including Idea 1/2/3's scoring and every "ruthless critique → fix" pair across every file — was generated by one process in one continuous pass, not two genuinely separate, blinded evaluations. That's a structurally weaker form of independence than this project's own `COUNCIL:CONVENE` protocol is explicitly built to provide (independent drafting, anonymization, blind peer review, only then a synthesis). **Not fixed retroactively** — re-running every prior audit through a genuinely blinded process isn't proportionate — but named as a standing limitation of this project's audit method going forward, and a real candidate for `COUNCIL:CONVENE` specifically on Themis Federal's highest-stakes remaining calls (which integrator to approach first, the wholesale pricing model).

**Checked, no violation found:** `SYCOPHANCY` (26) — no clear instance of a critique being softened because a direction seemed to please the person driving the project; the record includes multiple hard, evidence-based rejections (the $136B-crowded AI-safety-audit idea, the OneTrust-dominated cookie-consent idea) that a sycophantic process would have been more likely to wave through. `UNVERIFIED_SELF_CLAIM` (45) beyond Finding 2 — no further instance found where "this was verified" was stated without a shown check.

## 6. Final Verdict

Five of six loose ends closed structurally, using the same "make the bad state impossible to reach, not just checked for" discipline already proven on Themis Ledger's own core. The sixth (F6) was correctly left alone, and the reasoning for *why* is now explicit rather than assumed. The mapping that fed all of this checked out as accurate, with one real citation dropped for not actually being load-bearing. And the audit process itself — not just its subject — got checked, found two genuine, non-trivial issues (one now fixed with real sources, one named as a standing method limitation), and found no evidence of the softening `SYCOPHANCY` exists to catch. **Cleared, with the audit of the audit as part of the record, not a footnote.**
